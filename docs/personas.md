# Personas – NoVibe

NoVibe serves developers who struggle with drifting between ideas and not shipping consistently. These personas represent the core users and their motivations, frustrations, and desired outcomes.

---

## Persona 1: The Side Project Dev (PRIMARY PERSONA)

### Overview
A developer who loves starting new ideas but rarely finishes them. They juggle a job, life responsibilities, and bursts of creative inspiration. They value autonomy, minimal friction, and tools that help them move forward without feeling micromanaged.

### Behaviors
- Has 5–15 unfinished repos.
- Starts strong, loses momentum within days or weeks.
- Enjoys experimentation and "vibe coding."
- Uses GitHub regularly but sporadically.
- Avoids heavy project-management tools.

### Pain Points
- Difficulty turning ideas into shipped features.
- Lack of accountability leads to drift.
- Overwhelm or distraction when momentum breaks.
- Scattered attention across too many projects.

### What a Good Outcome After 4 Weeks Looks Like
- Sets a weekly intention all four weeks.
- Ships at least one small increment each week (e.g., a feature, refactor, bug fix, or improvement).
- Builds the habit of checking progress weekly.
- Feels clear about what they intend to do and whether they achieved it.
- Gains confidence from consistent, real movement instead of sporadic bursts.
- Has one project that moved from “stagnant” to “actively improving.”

---

## Persona 2: Early-Stage Founder Hacker

### Overview
A technical founder with multiple product ideas, building prototypes to explore viability. They care about speed and clarity but often drown in context switching.

### Behaviors
- Jumps between prototypes looking for a spark.
- Uses GitHub daily but not always intentionally.
- Keeps rough lists of goals (not structured issues).
- Wants signals that their time is going to the right project.

### Pain Points
- Hard to measure real progress.
- Too many competing ideas.
- Momentum collapses when alone.

### What They Want
A simple weekly signal that confirms whether they’re actually moving toward a prototype worth pursuing.

---

## Persona 3: Weekend Dabbler Dev

### Overview
A developer who codes mainly on weekends for fun. Has limited time and needs structure to make the most of it.

### Behaviors
- Works in 2–3 hour blocks on weekends.
- Commits irregularly but wants small wins.
- Doesn’t want complex workflows or tools.

### Pain Points
- Easy to lose the thread of a project week-to-week.
- Hard to remember what they intended to do next.
- Motivation drops when progress is unclear.

### What They Want
A simple reminder of what they planned and a sense of accomplishment when they follow through.

---

# Primary Persona Selection

**Primary Persona for MVP: The Side Project Dev**

Reasoning:
- Largest potential user base.
- Strongest pain around drift and unfinished work.
- Clear benefit from weekly intentions and GitHub-based accountability.
- Needs minimal structure, aligning perfectly with the MVP’s simplicity.

---

# Definition of Success After 4 Weeks (Primary Persona)

For the Side Project Dev, NoVibe is successful if:

- They’ve shown consistent weekly activity tied to their intentions.
- They shipped four small increments, no matter how modest.
- They regained a sense of momentum and direction.
- They experienced NoVibe as low-friction and encouraging.
- Their project feels “alive” rather than abandoned.

This early win sets the foundation for longer-term retention and habit-building.
